I've been taking things apart since before I knew what half the parts were called. Computers, mostly. A motherboard didn't mean anything to me the first time I pulled one out, I just wanted to see what was in there. Same thing with scripts later on, half the time I had no real goal, I just wanted to see what would happen if I ran it.

Cybersecurity is where that habit ended up. I didn't get into it chasing a paycheck, I got into it because it's basically the same game I was already playing: pull something apart, figure out why it broke, put it back together smarter than before.

A good chunk of my free time goes to HackTheBox, TryHackMe, and LetsDefend. I could say it's for the resume but honestly I'd probably still be doing it if nobody was hiring. Chasing an alert back to whatever actually caused it, or finally getting root on a box that's been kicking my ass for two days, scratches an itch nothing else does.

The homelab on this site is that same habit pointed at real infrastructure instead of a sandboxed VM. Real hardware, real alerts, and yes, I've broken it more than once and had to rebuild from scratch. Every post here is just me writing down what actually happened: what I tried, what didn't work, what finally did. Mostly so I don't have to relearn it in six months.

I'm still pretty green on the offensive side, not going to pretend otherwise. But understanding how an attack actually works changes how you think about defending against one, and that's worth the time it takes to get less bad at it.
